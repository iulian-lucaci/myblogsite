#### Title ####
Beyer - Sibiu, Hermannstadt - 1840

#### Description ####
This is an original engraving from the first half of the 19th century (created around 1820–1840) by the Viennese artist Leopold Beyer (1789–1877), bearing the signature "L. Beyer sc." (sculpsit – "engraved by") in the lower right corner.


Historical Context and Composition

• Original model. The piece is an engraved interpretation based on the famous panorama of Sibiu (Hermannstadt) created by the local painter Franz Neuhauser the Younger (around 1808–1818), an iconic composition in the city's iconography, also held in the collections of the Brukenthal National Museum.

• Perspective (View from the north). The city is captured from the Cibin River meadow (the area of today's Terezian / Gușterița neighborhoods), with the ridges of the Făgăraș and Cindrel Mountains in the background.

• Visible architectural landmarks. Along the skyline, the medieval towers and churches of the citadel are clearly visible: dominating the center-right is the tower of the Evangelical Church (with its four corner turrets), followed to the left by the Council Tower (Turnul Sfatului), the Roman Catholic Church, and the defensive towers of the city walls.

• Foreground details. In the foreground, the Cibin River is crossed by a wooden bridge, alongside a horse-drawn stagecoach on a country road, riders, and a few suburban homesteads.

#### Author: ####
Leopold Beyer (1789–1877) was a prominent Austrian copperplate and steel engraver, draughtsman, and graphic artist based in Vienna during the Biedermeier period.

Life and Background

• Birth and death. Born in Vienna on October 30, 1789 (some historical records also cite 1784), he spent his entire career in the Austrian capital, passing away there in November 1877.

• Historical context. Active during the first half of the 19th century, he worked at the height of the Austrian Empire's publishing boom, when illustrated travelogues, topographical albums, and historical encyclopedias were in high demand across Central Europe.

Artistic Career and Style

• Specialties. Beyer worked primarily in copper engraving (Kupferstich), steel engraving (Stahlstich), and lithography, prized for crisp, fine lines and delicate tonal shading.

• Notable collaborations. Like most master engravers of his era, he frequently translated on-site drawings and paintings by other celebrated artists into reproducible prints. He collaborated closely with leading figures such as Jakob Alt, Ludwig Richter, and Johann Adam Klein, as well as Viennese publishing houses like Artaria & Co..

• Main subjects. His body of work spans several distinct themes:

• Cityscapes (Vedute). Panoramic views of cities across the Habsburg territories and German states—including Vienna, Bamberg, Bayreuth, Hamburg, and Transylvanian towns like Hermannstadt (Sibiu).

• Military and historical scenes. Early in his career, he gained recognition for a 12-plate series depicting French Napoleonic troops (1813–1815) during the Wars of Liberation, capturing the everyday reality and hardship of soldiers on campaign.

• Portraits and book illustrations. He produced numerous engraved portraits of notable contemporaries—such as Samuel Hahnemann, founder of homeopathy and former personal physician to Baron Samuel von Brukenthal in Sibiu—alongside frontispieces for literary and religious works.

Legacy

• Museum collections. Today, Beyer's prints are preserved in major institutions—including the British Museum, the Wien Museum, and regional museums across Central and Eastern Europe—and remain sought after by collectors of 19th-century European topography.

Franz Neuhauser the Younger (1763–1836) was an Austrian-born painter, graphic artist, and drawing teacher who became the leading artistic figure in Transylvania at the turn of the 19th century, widely regarded as the founder of Transylvanian landscape painting.

Early Life and Move to Sibiu

• Viennese training. Born in Vienna in 1763 into a family of artists (his brother, Joseph Neuhauser, also became a noted painter in Cluj), Franz studied at the Academy of Fine Arts in Vienna, which grounded his work in Central European Baroque, Rococo, and early Biedermeier traditions.

• Arrival in Transylvania. Around 1780–1783, he settled in Sibiu (Hermannstadt), drawn by the vibrant cultural circle surrounding Baron Samuel von Brukenthal, Governor of Transylvania. He remained in Sibiu for the rest of his life, passing away there on August 22, 1836.

Artistic Contributions and Innovations

• Pioneer of landscape painting. Before Neuhauser, Transylvanian painting focused almost exclusively on portraits and religious commissions. He introduced independent landscape painting (vedute) and rural genre scenes, documenting the Carpathian mountains, Saxon fortified towns, and village life.

• Visual chronicler of Sibiu. His paintings serve as invaluable historical records of late 18th- and early 19th-century Sibiu:

• Sibiu in 1808 (Sibiul la 1808). His monumental panoramic oil painting from the north—showing the Cibin River valley, the medieval fortifications, and bustling townsfolk—became the definitive visual archetype of the city, copied and adapted by European engravers and lithographers (including Leopold Beyer and Jakob Alt) for decades.

• Annual Fair in Sibiu (Târg anual la Sibiu, 1789). A lively, detailed genre scene capturing Saxon townspeople, Romanian peasants, officers, and merchants in traditional attire.

• Lithography pioneer. Neuhauser was among the very first artists in Transylvania and present-day Romania to experiment with and introduce lithography, opening a new chapter in regional printmaking.

• Educator and collector. As a long-time drawing teacher in Sibiu, he shaped a generation of local artists. He was also an avid art collector; in 1834, the Brukenthal National Museum acquired 160 European prints from his personal collection to enrich its engraving cabinet.

Legacy

• Museum holdings. A large portion of his work—including landscapes, portraits of Transylvanian nobility, and mythological scenes—is preserved today at the Brukenthal National Museum in Sibiu, the Art Museum of Cluj-Napoca, and the Romanian Academy Library.
